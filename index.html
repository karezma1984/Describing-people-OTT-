3. Hobbies and Interests

I/You/We/They + like/enjoy + verb-ing · He/She/It + likes/enjoys + verb-ing · Negative: don't / doesn't + like + verb-ing.

4. Good at, Great at and Terrible at

These expressions describe someone's skill or ability in an activity. Use am/is/are, then good/great/terrible at, then a noun or verb-ing.

Terrible at means the person currently finds it difficult. It does not mean they cannot improve.

5–7. Present Simple and Present Continuous

Present Simple (habits, routines, likes, general facts): I/You/We/They play; He/She/It plays. Negative: doesn't + base verb (she doesn't like, not doesn't likes). Questions: Do/Does + base verb.

Present Continuous (now, at the moment): am/is/are + verb-ing. Negative: am not / isn't / aren't.

Third-person -s / -es / -ies: play → plays; watch → watches; study → studies (consonant + y → -ies); have → has.

-ing spelling: play → playing; make → making (drop e); bake → baking; run → running (double the consonant); sit → sitting; lie → lying (ie → ying).

Time words: Simple: every day, usually, often, sometimes, never. Continuous: now, right now, at the moment, Look!, Listen! Time words are clues; the whole sentence decides.

8. Comparative and Superlative Adjectives

Short adjectives: tall → taller → the tallest; small → smaller → the smallest; kind → kinder → the kindest.

Spelling: nice → nicer → the nicest; big → bigger → the biggest; happy → happier → the happiest.

Long adjectives: confident → more confident → the most confident; difficult → more difficult → the most difficult. Friendly → friendlier → the friendliest.

Irregular: good → better → the best; bad → worse → the worst. Never use double forms (not "more taller", not "the most tallest").
